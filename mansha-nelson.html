Taj's Local Tips: Mansha, Nelson

Tips shared by a regular local customer. These are personal experiences, not official Mansha instructions, confirmed offers or guaranteed prices.

Phone ahead when it's busy

Sometimes it's quicker to telephone your order in first, wait about 10 minutes, then go into the shop and tell the staff you've placed a telephone order and what you ordered. It can save a long wait compared with ordering when you arrive. Check with staff how collection and queuing work on the day.

A filling snack for around a fiver

A regular customer recommends asking about a combination of one samosa, one seekh kebab, two or three pakoras, salad, sauce and a roti or naan. They've found it can cost around £5 — good value for a filling meal. Ask what combinations and prices are available today.

Making curry at home? Pick up fresh bread

If you're cooking your own curry but don't fancy making the bread, a regular customer reports paying around £1 for one naan or £1 for two chapatis. They often buy 10 chapatis in one go because they make curry several times a week. Their extra tip? Ask if the chapatis can be made a little thinner if you prefer them softer and easier to eat.

Prices, portions, preparation requests and waiting times may change. Confirm directly with Mansha before ordering.

Understanding the food

Search the full food dictionary for explanations of 45 dishes and menu words.

New to South Asian food? Here is a plain-English guide to common menu words and dishes. This is a general glossary, not a list of items confirmed to be sold at Mansha. Recipes, ingredients and spice levels vary. Ask staff about allergens, meat types and availability.

Savoury snacks and starters

Samosa: Crispy pastry snack with different fillings, including vegetable, chicken and meat versions. Ask which fillings are available.

Pakora: Spiced battered snack, with varieties including vegetable and chicken pakoras.

Seekh kebab: Spiced minced meat shaped around a skewer and grilled. Varieties include chicken and lamb seekh kebabs, and cheesy chicken seekh kebabs.

Shami kebab: Soft, round patty usually made from minced meat, lentils and spices.

Chicken tikka: Marinated pieces of chicken cooked in a tandoor or on a grill.

Chaat: Tangy snack combining ingredients such as chickpeas, potato, yoghurt, chutney and crunchy toppings.

Chana chaat: Chickpea-based chaat with spices, chutney and often chopped vegetables.

Aloo tikki: Spiced potato patty, usually shallow-fried until golden.

Spring roll: Thin pastry roll with a savoury filling, fried until crisp.

Breads and sides

Chapati (roti): Soft, thin, unleavened flatbread, commonly eaten with curry.

Naan: Softer, thicker flatbread traditionally baked in a tandoor oven.

Paratha: Flatbread cooked with oil or ghee, often flaky and sometimes stuffed.

Keema naan: Naan stuffed with seasoned minced meat.

Peshwari naan: Naan with a sweet filling, often coconut, raisins and nuts.

Raita: Cooling yoghurt side, often mixed with cucumber, herbs or spices.

Chutney: Sauce or relish; mint, tamarind and chilli varieties can taste very different.

Achaar (pickle): Strongly flavoured, spiced pickle served in small amounts.

Curries and rice

Karahi: Curry cooked in a wok-like pan, often with tomato, ginger and spices.

Korma: Usually a mild, rich curry, sometimes made with yoghurt, cream or nuts.

Bhuna: A curry style where spices and sauce are cooked down for a thicker coating.

Daal: A dish made with lentils or split pulses, ranging from soupy to thick.

Chana masala: Spiced chickpea curry.

Keema: Minced meat cooked with spices, sometimes with peas or potatoes.

Biryani: Seasoned rice cooked or layered with meat or vegetables and aromatic spices.

Pilau rice: Rice cooked with spices and sometimes stock, vegetables or other additions.

Tandoori: Food cooked in a traditional clay oven, often marinated before cooking.

Sweets and desserts

Mithai: General name for South Asian sweets, often made with milk, sugar, flour, nuts or syrup.

Jalebi: Bright orange, spiral-shaped fried sweet soaked in syrup.

Gulab jamun: Soft round sweet soaked in fragrant sugar syrup.

Barfi: Dense milk-based sweet cut into squares or diamonds, sometimes flavoured with nuts.

Rasmalai: Soft cheese-based dumplings served in sweetened, flavoured milk.

Ladoo (laddu): Round sweet made from ingredients such as gram flour, semolina or lentils.

Peda: Small, soft milk-based sweet, often flavoured with cardamom.

Patisa (soan papdi): Light, flaky sweet that breaks into fine strands.

Halwa: Rich sweet dish that may be made from semolina, carrots or other ingredients.

Kheer: Creamy rice pudding often flavoured with cardamom and nuts.

Falooda: Cold dessert drink or sundae with vermicelli, syrup, milk and often ice cream.

Useful words when ordering

Aloo: Potato.

Chana: Chickpeas.

Murgh: Chicken.

Gosht: Meat; the specific type varies, so ask.

Sabzi: Vegetables or a vegetable dish.

Meetha: Sweet.

Teekha: Spicy or hot.

Mild: Usually less chilli heat, though spice levels vary between kitchens.
