Visiting Nelson for Food?

A simple guide for first-time visitors, people new to South Asian food and anyone who wants to discover Nelson's local favourites.

Plan your food visit in four steps

  1. Choose a takeaway. Explore the Pendle takeaway directory or use the Nelson food map to find a shop.
  2. Understand the food. Look up unfamiliar dishes in the plain-English food dictionary. Ask about ingredients, allergens and spice levels.
  3. Read local tips. Check individual takeaway pages for first-hand customer advice on popular dishes, ordering and value.
  4. Check before you travel. Telephone the shop to confirm opening times, prices, availability, waiting times and collection arrangements.

What should I order?

Not sure where to begin? Samosas and pakoras are savoury snacks; seekh kebabs are spiced minced meat cooked on skewers; chapatis and naan are breads commonly eaten with curries.

How spicy is it?

Heat varies between kitchens. Ask whether a dish is mild, medium or hot and whether it can be made less spicy. Not every curry is hot.

If you have a food allergy, ask staff directly about ingredients and cross-contact before ordering.

How do I order?

Some shops take telephone orders for collection. When you arrive, tell staff you placed a telephone order and give the name or order details requested. Follow the shop's queue and collection instructions.

Don't assume that a reported waiting time applies on every visit.

What if I don't know the language?

It's fine to ask staff in English what a dish contains or how it is served. You can show the name of a dish on your phone, point to the menu or ask for a recommendation.

Useful words: aloo means potato, chana means chickpeas, and meetha means sweet.

Eating on a budget

Ask whether a curry includes rice or bread, and whether salad and sauce are included. Reported prices and combinations can change, so confirm the full price before paying.

Local recommendations can help you spot good-value meals.

Getting there

Use the map to open directions to individual takeaways. If you're driving, check current parking restrictions and signs when you arrive. Do not assume a particular car park is free.
